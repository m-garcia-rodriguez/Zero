Every decision below is applied in queries/ and analysis/. Update this file whenever one changes.

#1  DATA SOURCE
    A998 fluency test only:
      bi_gold_prod.dm_research.fluency_test_statements  (one row per statement)
      bi_gold_prod.dm_research.fluency_test_metrics     (joined on metric_id, gives course age)
    The Fluency zone (dwh_digital_practice.f_elementary_fluency_attempts) is NOT used for now.
    Reason: A998 is a controlled, timed test; the Fluency zone is self-paced and noisier.

#2  ACADEMIC YEAR / COUNTRY
    One academic_year_id per figure, never pooled. Built so far: ES_2025, ES_2024. Italy was built and then dropped (see #12).
    The year is a marker in the .sql ("-- {YEAR}"): the notebook substitutes it and passes
    sql_text= to cached_query(), so each year gets its own cache entry.
    Note: ES_2024 is thin at age 15 (~3k responses, 84 of 100 facts survive the >=30 rule).

#3  OPERATIONS INCLUDED
    Multiplication only, matched with RLIKE '^[0-9][×·][0-9]$'.
    A998 also contains division (e.g. '48:6'), which is excluded.
    Facts are single-digit: 0-9 x 0-9, i.e. 100 possible facts.

#4  GLYPH NORMALISATION
    A998 renders multiplication two ways: '7×3' (ages 8-11) and '7·3' (ages 12-15, activity pack 39).
    '·' is normalised to '×' and the two are pooled, so one fact is one point.
    Reason: same 100 facts, different rendering by school stage. Keeping them apart would remove
    secondary-school ages from the study entirely. (Same convention as the MULTIPLY repo.)

#5  DIRECTIONALITY
    Facts are DIRECTIONAL ordered pairs: 0×7 and 7×0 are two different points, never averaged.
    Reason: order asymmetry is one of the things the zero question is about.

#6  AGE GROUPING
    Panels are split by m.classroom_course_age (the classroom's course age, not the student's own
    age), restricted to 8-15. NULLs and ages 5-7 are dropped (<1k statements each, noise).
    Panels are labelled with the age alone ("Age 8"), not with the school year.

#7  FAST-WRONG FILTER (the 1.2 s rule)
    A response is dropped when it is NOT Correct AND was answered in under 1.2 s.
    It removes the individual RESPONSE, not the student: a student with one anticipation keeps
    all their other responses.
    Applied to both Incorrect and Help responses (everything counted as an error under #8).
    Reason: sub-1.2 s wrong answers are anticipations or mis-taps, not retrieval attempts.

#8  HELP STATEMENTS
    statement_result = 'Help' COUNTS AS AN ERROR.
    error_rate = (Incorrect + Help) / all kept responses.
    Reason: asking for help is a failure to retrieve the fact. The query also returns n_help
    separately, so the size of this choice can be checked at any time.

#9  RESPONSE TIME
    RT = statement_seconds_spent, MEDIAN over CORRECT responses only.
    Reason: median is robust to the long right tail; wrong answers mix fast guesses with slow
    struggles, so their RT is not comparable.

#10 RT OUTLIERS
    No upper cut-off. The median already absorbs the tail, so nothing is discarded.

#11 MINIMUM SAMPLE
    A fact x age cell is plotted only with >= 30 kept responses (HAVING count(*) >= 30).
    In practice every surviving cell has far more (minimum observed: ~500).

#12 KNOWN GAPS -- COVERAGE OF THE 0-FACTS
    Age 8 (both countries, both years): the test administers neither the 0-facts nor the 1-facts.
    72 of the 100 facts appear and none contains a 0. The panel is annotated in the figure.
    Italy: dropped from the study. IT_2025 only reached ages 8-10 after the >=30 rule and IT_2024
    only ages 8-9 with no 0-facts at all; across all years only 2.8% of Italian responses involve
    a 0. None of this is a filtering artefact: those items are absent from the source data.
    Country coverage is measured by queries/country_coverage.sql (first cell of the notebook):
    ES 11.1M responses (11.9% with a 0), MX 1.39M (10.0%), IT 580k (2.8%), US 138k (3.1%),
    EC-SI 128k, CO-B 84k, CL 35k, EC-CO 30k, BR 11k, CO-A 9k. Only ES, and possibly MX, carry
    enough 0-fact data for this study.

2026-09-21  Decisions #14-#17 (second notebook: what the wrong answer is)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------

#14 ERROR TAXONOMY ON THE 0-FACTS
    Every wrong answer to a fact containing a 0 falls in exactly one class, priority order:
      "= n (additive)"  the answer equals a+b, i.e. the non-zero operand   (0x7 -> 7)
      "= 1"             the answer is 1                                   (multiplicative identity)
      "Other digit"     the answer is 2-9 and is not n
      ">= 10"           the answer is 10 or more
    Reason: on the 0-facts the answer space collapses to the single digits -- in ES_2025 exactly
    one of 54,804 errors is a two-digit answer -- so the classic operand / table / non-table
    taxonomy has nothing to separate. These classes are what the data actually contains.
    "= n" is the additive error the study reports. "= 1" is kept apart because it grows with age
    (3% of answered errors at age 9, 8-18% at 14-15, on a few hundred responses) and is a
    different rule, not a mis-tap.
    Answer column: fluency_test_statements.user_answer, parsed with try_cast. Leading zeros are
    common ('098') and parse fine; a non-numeric click string occurs twice in the whole year and
    is counted as no answer.

#15 0x0 IS KEPT APART
    0x0 is excluded from the 0-fact panels and from every reported %. Its correct answer is 0 and
    a+b = 0 as well, so the additive error is undefined for it. It is reported on its own line of
    the coverage table (error rate 0.7-4.2%, far below the other 0-facts).

#16 HELP / NO ANSWER IN THE ERROR ANALYSIS
    Help still counts as an error in the error RATE (#8 unchanged), but the histogram and the type
    mix use ANSWERED errors only: a Help leaves no answer to classify. In ES_2025 that is 0.4% of
    the 0-fact errors, so the choice moves nothing. Counted separately in the coverage table.

#17 DENOMINATORS FOR THE REPORTED %
    The headline number is the share of ALL RESPONSES to the fact that are answered "n"
    (pct_of_all_responses): answering n is never correct on a 0-fact, so this is the share of the
    whole response volume lost to this one mistake. Reported next to it, not instead of it, is the
    share of the ANSWERED ERRORS that are "= n" (pct_of_answered_errors), which says what an error
    looks like once one is made. Directions (0xn, nx0) are never pooled (#5).

2026-09-21  Decisions #18-#21 (third notebook: RT distribution by direction)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------

#18 POPULATION OF THE RT DISTRIBUTION
    One row per RESPONSE (not per fact x age cell): every CORRECT answer to a fact containing a 0,
    ES_2025, ages 8-15. Query: queries/rt_zero_facts_correct.sql.
    The >= 30 rule (#11) counts ALL responses, so it cannot be applied to a correct-only pull:
    the notebook takes the surviving (age, operation) cells from queries/rt_error_by_fact_age.sql
    and keeps only responses in those cells. In ES_2025 this drops 31 of 474,989 responses and
    removes age 8 entirely -- the test administers no 0-facts there (#12).
    Only correct responses (#9): wrong answers mix fast guesses with slow struggles.

#19 THE DIRECTION COMPARISON IS PAIRED WITHIN STUDENT
    Responses are nested in students and the same student meets both directions in the same test,
    so 0xn vs nx0 is tested paired, not as two independent samples: one median RT per student and
    direction, then the Wilcoxon signed-rank test on the per-student difference nx0 - 0xn.
    Rank-based, not parametric: the per-student differences are near-symmetric (skew -0.3) but extremely
    heavy-tailed (excess kurtosis ~100, range -103 to +111 s), so a mean-based test is dominated by a
    handful of students. The paired t test is reported alongside as a robustness check only; it agrees on
    the direction (p = .002 pooled) but is far weaker than the rank test (p < .001), which is that tail
    dominance. Effect size: matched-pairs rank-biserial correlation.
    Seven ages are tested, so p values are also reported Holm-corrected family-wise; all seven survive.
    Pooled over all 70,735 students: Mdn(nx0 - 0xn) = 34 ms, 95% CI [33, 42], 52.9% of students slower
    on nx0, p < .001, r_rb = .058. CI of the median difference from the order statistics
    (distribution-free), not bootstrapped -- it assumes nothing about the shape and is exact.
    Students with only one of the two directions at a given age are dropped from the test
    (70,735 student x age pairs survive out of 104,846 students).

#20 VIOLIN AXIS IS A DISPLAY WINDOW, NOT A FILTER
    The violins are drawn on a linear y-axis fixed by RT_LIM in the notebook (currently 0-4 s; 11.9% of
    the correct responses are above it). Correct RT is strongly right-skewed (median ~2 s, maximum
    ~110 s), so the full range flattens the body of the distribution into a line. The window is DISPLAY
    only: the kernel, the quartile lines and every reported number use all responses, and #10 (no upper
    cut-off) is unchanged. A log-axis variant showing the whole range, tail included, is saved next to
    it as *_log.pdf/.png.
    The KDE is drawn with gridsize=1000, not seaborn's default 100: the grid spans the whole data range
    (0.6-110 s), so 100 points is one every 1.1 s and the body of the distribution (1-5 s) came out as a
    straight-edged triangle. This is a rendering setting, not a filter.

#21 0x0 IN THE RT FIGURE
    0x0 is excluded from the two series, consistent with #15: it belongs to neither direction.
    It is reported on its own line of the coverage table (it is the fastest of the 0-facts at
    every age).

2026-09-21  Decisions #22-#25 (fourth notebook: order asymmetry, 0-facts vs every pair)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------

#22 THE COMPARISON IS GENERALISED TO ALL 45 PAIRS
    The nx0 vs 0xn test (#19) is run on every pair of DIFFERENT operands, 45 in total (C(10,2)).
    Squares (axa) are excluded: they have no second order. Query: queries/rt_by_fact_correct.sql,
    a superset of queries/rt_zero_facts_correct.sql with the same population and filters.
    Within a pair the operands are ordered small-first / large-first, so 2x3 is small-first and 3x2
    large-first; the difference is always large-first minus small-first, which for a 0-fact is
    nx0 - 0xn. The zero is the smallest operand, so the 0-facts keep the sign convention of #19.

#23 AGES POOLED WITHIN A PAIR, HOLM OVER THE 45 PAIRS
    The per-pair test pools ages: the pairing is within student and a student sits in one course, so
    each student contributes one difference per pair. 45 pairs are one family -> Holm correction.
    Difficulty of a pair is its ERROR RATE over all responses (from queries/rt_error_by_fact_age.sql),
    not its RT: using RT to rank difficulty and then explaining an RT effect with it would be circular.

#24 THE ORDER ASYMMETRY IS A RULE EFFECT, NOT A ZERO EFFECT AND NOT A DIFFICULTY EFFECT
    (Rewritten 2026-09-21. The first version of this decision concluded "easy-fact effect". That was
    wrong: the comparison group for the 0-facts was dominated by the 1-facts, which are not ordinary
    easy facts -- n×1 = n is a RULE exactly as n×0 = 0 is. Corrected grouping below.)

    Three groups of pairs: rule-0 (the 9 pairs with a zero), rule-1 (the 8 pairs 1x2..1x9), and
    retrieval (the 28 pairs of operands 2-9, which have to be recalled from the table).
    Result, ES_2025, large-first minus small-first:
      rule-0      9 pairs  r_rb Mdn +.051  +41 ms  large-first slower in  9/9   error Mdn 10.4%
      rule-1      8 pairs  r_rb Mdn +.071  +42 ms  large-first slower in  7/8   error Mdn  6.0%
      retrieval  28 pairs  r_rb Mdn -.029  -51 ms  large-first slower in  9/28  error Mdn 25.1%
    Kruskal-Wallis over the three groups H = 20.8, p < .001. The two rule tables are indistinguishable
    from each other (U = 25, p = .321); rule against retrieval U = 433, p < .001.

    Difficulty does NOT explain it, though it looked like it did before the 1-facts were separated:
      - rho(asymmetry, error rate) = -.61 over all 45 pairs but +.03 (p = .87) within the 28 retrieval
        pairs. The apparent trend was the rule facts being both easy and asymmetric.
      - The Theil-Sen trend fitted on the retrieval pairs alone is flat (slope +.0006). Rule facts sit
        above it: residual Mdn +.098 vs -.001, p < .001.
      - Against the 9 retrieval pairs that are equally easy (< 20% errors: 2x3..2x9, 3x4, 3x5, 3x7) the
        rule facts are still different, p = .002; those easy retrieval pairs show no asymmetry at all
        (r_rb Mdn -.011, large-first slower in only 3 of 9).

    Reading: when the fact is solved by applying a rule, meeting the rule operand FIRST (0xn, 1xn) is
    faster than meeting it second (nx0, nx1) -- the rule can fire as soon as the first operand is read.
    When the fact has to be retrieved, there is no such trigger and the order effect is not systematic.
    The 0 is one of two rule tables and behaves like the other, so the paper's claim is about rule
    application, not about the zero on its own. The 1-facts are the natural control group and belong
    in the study.

#25 WHAT IS NOT CONTROLLED YET
    Presentation order inside the test (whether one order tends to be administered earlier in the
    session, which would carry a warm-up effect) is not modelled. Nor is the activity pack.

2026-09-21  Decisions #26-#27 (replication in ES_2024)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------

#26 THE PAIR ANALYSIS RUNS ON TWO YEARS, INDEPENDENTLY
    rt_direction_by_pair.ipynb runs the whole analysis once per academic year (ES_2025, ES_2024),
    never pooled (#2). ES_2024: 2.25M correct responses, 156k students, all 45 pairs clear the >= 30
    rule, smallest pair 2,954 students.
    The "equally easy retrieval pairs" control is ranked WITHIN the year (the 17 easiest retrieval
    pairs, matching the 17 rule pairs in count). A fixed threshold does not transfer: ES_2024 is
    harder overall and has NO retrieval pair under 20% errors, which silently emptied the control.
    Note this control is weak in both years -- no retrieval pair is as easy as a rule fact
    (rule error Mdn ~10%, the 17 easiest retrieval pairs ~19% in 2025, ~30% in 2024). The Theil-Sen
    residual control is the one that carries the argument.

#27 WHAT REPLICATES AND WHAT DOES NOT
    ROBUST across both years:
      - The 0-fact asymmetry: large-first (nx0) slower in 9 of 9 pairs both years,
        Mdn +41 ms (2025) / +49 ms (2024), r_rb .035-.075 / .036-.084.
      - The 1-table behaves like the 0-table: 0 vs 1 not different (p = .321 / .200), 7 of 8 pairs
        positive both years -- and the SAME two exceptions both years: 1x2 negative (-.064 / -.042)
        and 1x3 flat (+.001 / +.017). Both are the smallest operands.
      - Rule facts sit above the retrieval difficulty trend: residual Mdn +.098 / +.092,
        p < .001 in both years.
      - No difficulty gradient inside the retrieval pairs: rho +.03 (p = .87) / +.06 (p = .75).
      - Magnitudes: rule benefit +1558/+1782 ms (0) and +1457/+1569 ms (1), r_rb .80-.84;
        order effect 33-43 ms, 2.1-2.7% of the benefit, r_rb .042-.075.
      - Per-pair effect sizes correlate across years: Spearman rho = +.69 (45 pairs); all 17 rule
        pairs keep their sign, 36 of 45 pairs overall.
      - Strongly negative pairs replicate: 3x5, 3x6, 4x5, 4x6, 3x7, 2x5 (large-first FASTER).
      - 2x3 and 2x4 are positive and Holm-significant in both years: retrieval pairs that behave
        like rule facts. Small operands again.

    NOT ROBUST:
      - The uncontrolled rule-vs-retrieval contrast weakens: p < .001 (2025) -> p = .030 (2024),
        and the three-group Kruskal falls to p = .051. The reason is a year-level SHIFT of the
        retrieval group toward positive (median r_rb -.029 -> -.001; e.g. 5x6 +.002 -> +.097,
        5x8 +.001 -> +.067, 2x6 -.011 -> +.067). The rule groups do not move.
      - The overall difficulty gradient over all 45 pairs: rho -.61 -> -.30.
      - 6x8, the retrieval outlier of 2025 (+117 ms), does not replicate (+82 ms, Holm p = 1.00).
      - Only 13 of 45 pairs are Holm-significant AND same-signed in both years.

    Reading: the RULE effect replicates, the DIFFICULTY story does not and was never the mechanism.
    The year-level shift of the retrieval group is the strongest argument yet for #25: whatever moves
    between years (item presentation order within the session, activity packs) touches the retrieval
    facts and leaves the rule facts alone. That must be modelled before the paper claims a mechanism.

2026-09-21  Decisions #29-#33 (fourth notebook: is the order effect real at the student level)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------

#29 WHAT THE DESIGN CAN AND CANNOT SUPPORT
    A998 is a fluency test: the median student contributes 3 correct trials per condition (75th pct 3,
    max 15; 45,615 of 59,175 students have at least one in both). No analysis can recover a reliable
    INDIVIDUAL order effect from that. What the data can establish is whether the effect is a
    population-level WITHIN-STUDENT shift that does not depend on who the student is. Every claim in
    rt_order_robustness.ipynb is written at that level.

#30 TRIAL-LEVEL INFERENCE: FIXED EFFECTS, CLUSTERED BY STUDENT
    Trials are nested in students and facts and are never treated as independent.
    Primary model: log(RT) ~ nx0, student and fact (the non-zero operand) absorbed as fixed effects,
    standard errors clustered by student (pyfixest). Log because RT is right-skewed and a log effect is
    a PROPORTIONAL one, which also answers "do slower students just show bigger millisecond gaps".
    Effects are converted back to ms at the observed 0xn median (2.033 s in ES_2025).
    Secondary: a mixed model with random intercept AND random slope of order by student, fitted on a
    random subsample of 20,000 students (the full 59k does not fit in the machine's memory).
    Query: queries/rt_zero_facts_trials.sql (correct AND incorrect responses, so accuracy shares it).

#31 THE STUDENT CONTROLS ARE MEASURED OUTSIDE THE ZERO PROBLEMS
    student_level = accuracy over all kept responses to facts with NO zero operand.
    student_speed = mean log RT over the correct ones (HIGHER = SLOWER).
    Both standardised within age, so they carry no age information; both require >= 20 non-zero trials,
    which is what reduces 112,409 students to 59,175. Query: queries/student_controls.sql, which also
    returns the same measures on the non-rule facts only (operands 2-9), since 1-facts are rule facts
    too (#24) and arguably do not belong in a general-proficiency baseline. The primary controls use
    the "no zero operand" version, as specified.

#32 RESULT: THE EFFECT IS A WITHIN-STUDENT SHIFT, INDEPENDENT OF WHO THE STUDENT IS
    Six estimators with different assumptions, all in ms at the 0xn median:
      trial FE, student + fact          +39.9 [32.7, 47.1]   t = 11.0, p < .001 (+1.96% in log)
      trial FE, all 112k students       +38.2 [31.8, 44.6]   (no control merge -> no selection)
      mixed model, random slope         +32.2 [20.8, 43.6]
      student delta, median             +34.0 [33.0, 42.0]   45,615 students, 53.4% above zero
      matched student x operand         +34.0 [29.0, 43.0]   27,375 students, same fact both orders
      one trial each, 200 resamples     +34.0 [33.0, 48.0]   -> not driven by heavy users
    By age the trial FE estimate is 36.6 to 55.1 ms, every CI above zero, ages 9-15.
    NO moderation: order x age p = .199, order x level p = .252, order x speed p = .440. The same three
    regressions on the per-student delta are all null. The random slope VARIANCE is ~0 (SD .007 log
    against a fixed effect of .016), i.e. no detectable between-student variability in the effect --
    though with 3 trials per condition that test has little power, so read it as "no evidence of
    heterogeneity", not as "the effect is identical in every student".
    Mean-based statistics are much weaker than rank/median ones (t = 3.7 vs Wilcoxon p < 1e-40 on the
    student deltas) -- the heavy tails of #19 again. The median is the estimate to quote.

#33 ACCURACY GOES THE SAME WAY -- NO SPEED-ACCURACY TRADE-OFF
    nx0 is not only slower but also less accurate: 89.86% vs 92.22% raw, -2.4 percentage points within
    student and fact (t = -26.4, p < .001), odds ratio 0.73 [0.72, 0.75].
    Unlike the RT effect, the accuracy gap DOES grow with age (order x age -0.31 points per year,
    p < .001): -0.8 points at age 9, -3.5 at age 12, -1.4 at 15. It does not depend on proficiency
    (p = .790) or speed (p = .183).
    So the order effect is a plain disadvantage for nx0 on both outcomes, which is what a rule-first
    account predicts and a speed-accuracy trade-off does not.

#34 HOW TRIAL ORDER AND DISTANCE ARE MEASURED
    fluency_test_statements stores statement_idx (position inside the metric) but only ONE
    response_at per METRIC -- every statement of a test carries the test's timestamp, checked:
    399,581 metrics, 399,581 distinct (metric, timestamp) pairs. So elapsed time inside a test is
    not recorded and three measures are emitted instead (queries/sequential_trials.sql):
      pos_all      position among ALL of the student's statements, ordered by
                   (response_at, metric_id, statement_idx). Divisions and trials dropped by the
                   1.2 s rule are counted: they consumed test time even though they are not
                   analysed. trials_between = difference - 1.
      cum_seconds  sum of statement_seconds_spent inside the metric up to that statement. For two
                   trials of the SAME test their difference is the elapsed answering time between
                   them (median 21 s, p90 59 s). Undefined across tests.
      metric_seq   which test of that student this is, so same-test and across-test pairs separate.
    Across tests the distance is the difference of response_at in days (median 102, max 199).

#35 THE UNIT OF ANALYSIS IS THE STUDENT, SEs CLUSTERED BY CLASSROOM
    The primary analysis uses each student's chronologically FIRST TWO kept ×0 trials at a given
    course age, one pair per student. Reason: a student with 14 trials would otherwise dominate a
    student with 2, and the number of trials is itself a function of how much of the test they did.
    Because the row is a student and no longer a trial, the trial-level clustering of the other
    notebooks does not apply; SEs are clustered by CLASSROOM, since students sit the same test in
    the same room on the same day (9,230 classrooms, no student in more than one --
    queries/student_classroom.sql). Ages are never pooled inside a pair: both trials are at the
    same classroom_course_age.
    Trial 1 and Trial 2 are the first two trials that SURVIVE the filters (#7, #11), re-ranked in
    the notebook after filtering, not the first two raw trials. Trials dropped by the filters still
    count toward trials_between (#34).

#36 PROBLEM TYPES: ×0, ×1 AND RETRIEVAL
    Following #24, "ordinary multiplication" for the control analysis is RETRIEVAL (both operands
    2-9) and the 1-table is a third group, not part of the control: n×1 = n is a rule exactly as
    n×0 = 0, and pooling it into the baseline is the error #24 had to correct.
      zero       exactly one operand is 0      499,834 kept trials, 112,409 students
      one        no zero, at least one 1       668,684 trials, 159,134 students
      retrieval  both operands 2-9           2,046,472 trials (capped, #37), 171,502 students
    0×0 is excluded (#15) and 1×1 is excluded for the same reason (it belongs to neither order and
    its own rule is degenerate): 28,064 and 27,982 trials.
    0×1 and 1×0 belong to BOTH rule families. They are counted as ×0, because the zero rule is what
    determines the answer. Consequence: the ×0 family contains two facts that are also 1-facts and
    the ×1 family contains none that are 0-facts. Not yet checked as a robustness variant.

#37 THE FIRST 14 TRIALS OF A TYPE PER STUDENT
    queries/sequential_trials.sql returns only a student's first 14 kept trials of each type at each
    age (marker -- {CAP}). 14 is the 99th percentile of the ×0 count per student (median 4); the cap
    therefore removes 0.4% of ×0 trials and 41% of retrieval trials, whose median is 18 and maximum
    122. Without it the problem-type comparison of #36 would partly be a comparison of sequence
    lengths, and the pull would be 5.4M rows instead of 3.2M.

#38 ASSOCIATION MEASURES (binary_stats.py)
    Per probability: Wilson score interval. Difference of two probabilities: Newcombe hybrid-score
    interval -- a Wald interval on a difference of ~93% vs ~52% misbehaves. Odds ratio with the
    Woolf log interval.
    The headline measure for comparing PROBLEM TYPES is the TETRACHORIC correlation, with a
    percentile bootstrap over students. Reason: the odds ratio is not comparable across tables with
    different marginals, and the three types differ a lot in base accuracy (×1 94.6%, ×0 93.3%,
    retrieval 83.5%), so an OR comparison would partly be a comparison of base rates. The
    tetrachoric assumes a latent bivariate normal and is invariant to where the thresholds fall.

#39 A998 GIVES NO FEEDBACK AFTER A WRONG ANSWER
    Confirmed with the product team: no correction, no correct answer shown, no retry prompt, no
    rule reminder. Trial 1 correctness therefore does NOT come with a difference in instruction
    received, which removes the usual confound of a sequential design.
    The data agree that there is no implicit adaptive response either: after an error the next ×0
    item is the same directed fact 1.38% of the time against 1.18% after a correct answer, at the
    same median distance (6 items). A directed fact does repeat inside a test in ~3% of
    (metric, fact) cases, but not as a response to failing it.

#40 NO STUDENT FIXED EFFECT, AND NO RANDOM INTERCEPT, ON A LAGGED OUTCOME
    The predictor is a lagged value of the outcome. With a handful of trials per student a student
    fixed effect is estimated from those same outcomes and the within estimator is biased downward
    by roughly -1/(T-1) (Nickell). Fitted here it returns -4.9 pp, i.e. the sign flips: that is the
    bias, not a result. It is kept in the notebook, labelled, so nobody fits it again.
    A student random intercept is the milder version of the same problem -- it partials out exactly
    the between-student stability the association consists of, and the initial-conditions problem
    contaminates it. It is also not estimable at this scale with what is installed: a variational
    BinomialBayesMixedGLM over 55k student intercepts does not settle (its OR moved between 6.7 and
    10.4 across subsamples) and variational posterior SDs are understated by construction. The
    all-transitions model reported is a population-averaged GEE with an exchangeable working
    correlation within student.
    CONSEQUENCE FOR THE PAPER: this design cannot separate stable between-student differences from
    genuine trial-to-trial carry-over (the heterogeneity vs state-dependence problem). What it CAN
    support is the comparisons -- across problem types, across transfer conditions, across temporal
    distance -- where the same structure sits on both sides.

#44 ERROR TAXONOMY ON THE 1-FACTS -- "COPIES THE OTHER OPERAND"
    Both rules return one of the operands (n×0 -> 0, n×1 -> n). The 0-fact error "= n" returns the wrong one;
    its mirror on the 1-facts is "= 1". Classes on the 1-facts, priority order: "= 1 (other operand)",
    "= n+1 (additive)", "= 0 (zero rule)", "Other". On a 0-fact "= n" is at the same time the additive, the
    copy and the 1-rule answer and cannot be split; on a 1-fact the three are different answers.
    RESULT (ES_2025, ages 9-15, 0x1/1x0 apart): copying the other operand is 8.5% of ALL 0-fact responses
    (79% of answered errors) vs 1.4% of 1-fact responses (29%). The purely additive answer n+1 is 0.8% of
    1-fact responses (17% of errors). Cross-rule intrusions are small and symmetric: "= 1" on 0-facts 4.6%
    of errors, "= 0" on 1-facts 5.6%. 0x1 / 1x0 errors are answered 1 in 87% of cases.
    Reading: the dominant 0-fact error is not a generic additive slip (that is rare on the 1-facts) and not a
    generic operand-copy (5-6x rarer on the 1-facts). It is specific to the zero.

#45 MULTIPLICATION SIGNS (reviewer question 1) -- queries/check_operation_glyphs.sql

#46 HELP AND MIS-RECORDED ANSWERS (reviewer questions 2 and 3) -- queries/check_user_answer_quality.sql
